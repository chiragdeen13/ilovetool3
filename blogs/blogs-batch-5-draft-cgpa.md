## Body

Your result card shows a CGPA. The application form in front of you asks for a percentage. And the moment you search for the conversion, you meet two different answers for the same number — which is how an 8.5 CGPA becomes 85% on one website and 80.75% on another.

Both answers can be right, because there is no single worldwide CGPA-to-percentage formula. The conversion depends on your grading scale and, most importantly, on the rule of the institution that issued your CGPA. This guide explains the formulas honestly, works through real examples, and shows you how to convert in seconds with a free calculator.

### The two formulas you will meet

**1. The scale formula (the general one).** Percentage = (CGPA ÷ scale) × 100. On a 10-point scale this is the same as multiplying by 10; on a 4-point scale you divide by 4 and multiply by 100; on a 5-point scale you divide by 5 and multiply by 100. This is the main result most converters — including the calculator linked below — will give you.

**2. The CBSE formula (a specific one).** India's CBSE board converts its own CGPA with Percentage = CGPA × 9.5. It applies to CBSE results — not automatically to every 10-point CGPA from every university. That is why an 8.5 gives 85.00% by the scale formula but 80.75% by the CBSE formula (8.5 × 9.5).

Neither formula is "the fake one". They belong to different systems, and the right one for an official form is always the one your own institution prescribes.

### Worked examples

Every example below was checked against the free calculator on this site:

- **CGPA 8.5 out of 10** → 85.00% by the scale formula; 80.75% by the CBSE formula.

- **CGPA 4.2 out of 5** → 84.00%.
- **CGPA 10 out of 10** → 100.00% by the scale formula; the CBSE formula gives 95.00%, because CBSE's ×9.5 tops out there by design.

### Which number should you put on an application form?

Use this order:

1. **Your institution's own formula wins.** Many universities print their conversion rule on the transcript or website. If they give one, use it — even if it differs from both formulas above.
2. **CBSE results use ×9.5.** If the CGPA is from CBSE (Class 9–10), the board's own conversion is CGPA × 9.5.
3. **Otherwise, the scale formula is the common default** — (CGPA ÷ scale) × 100 — for general purposes like comparing scores or filling an informal form.

When a form matters — admissions, a job application, a scholarship — a converted figure that used the wrong rule can look like an inflated claim. Two minutes spent finding the institution's rule is worth it.

### Common mistakes to avoid

- **Using ×9.5 on a 4-point GPA.** The CBSE multiplier belongs to the 10-point scale. A 3.2 out of 4 is 80.00% by the scale formula — not 3.2 × 9.5.
- **Assuming ×10 is universal.** It is common, not official everywhere. Institutions may use ×9.5 or their own grade table.
- **Converting a single semester's GPA and calling it CGPA.** CGPA is the cumulative average across semesters; converting one term's GPA gives a percentage for that term only. (If you need the GPA itself first, see our guide to [calculating your GPA](https://ilovetool3.com/how-to-calculate-your-gpa-with-a-worked-example-on-the-4-0-scale/).)
- **Rounding aggressively.** Keep the two decimal places the calculator gives you; rounding 80.75% up to 81% on an official form is a small inaccuracy you do not need.

### Frequently asked questions

**Is CGPA × 10 always correct?**
No. It is the scale formula for a 10-point CGPA and it is widely used, but it is not universal. CBSE uses ×9.5 for its own results, and many universities publish their own conversion tables. For official use, follow your institution.

**Why does the calculator show two results on the 10-point scale?**

**Does the CBSE line appear for the 4 and 5 point scales?**
No. In the calculator it appears only when the scale is Out of 10, because the CBSE formula is a 10-point-scale rule.

No. The conversion runs entirely in your browser — nothing is stored or transmitted.
