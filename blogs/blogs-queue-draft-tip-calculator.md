How to Calculate a Tip and Split the Bill Fairly

The bill arrives, everyone reaches for their phone, and suddenly a simple dinner becomes a maths problem. How much tip is fair? What is the total? And if four people shared the meal, what does each person owe? Guessing leads to the two classic failures: the server is short-changed, or one friend quietly overpays. A tip calculation has three answers — the tip, the total bill, and each person's share — and getting all three right takes less than a minute. This guide shows how, with real examples tested on our free [Tip Calculator](https://ilovetool3.com/tip-calculator/).

## The formula, in plain words

The tip is the bill multiplied by the tip percentage divided by 100. The total bill is the original bill plus the tip. The per-person share is the total bill divided by the number of people. Written as formulas:

- Tip Amount = Bill Amount × (Tip % ÷ 100)
- Total Bill = Bill Amount + Tip Amount
- Per Person = Total Bill ÷ Number of People

A useful shortcut: 10% of any bill is the bill with the decimal point moved one place left, so 10% of 4,000 is 400. Double it for 20%, halve it for 5%, and combine the pieces for anything in between.

## Common mistakes to avoid

**Splitting the bill before adding the tip.** If each person tips on their own share separately, rounding at each step can leave the server short. Calculate the tip on the full bill first, then split the total — that is the order the calculator uses, and it keeps the full tip intact.

**Using an equal split when orders were very unequal.** The tool page warns about this directly: "For an uneven split, calculate each person's share separately rather than using an equal split." If one person ordered far more than the rest, an equal split is not fair. Use the calculator for the shared items, and settle the uneven items separately.

**Tipping on the wrong base without saying so.** Some people tip on the pre-tax amount and others on the total including tax. The tool page notes that either is common, and advises being consistent and telling the group which base you used if others are splitting with you. A quiet disagreement about the base is a common source of a shortfall.

**Forgetting that the percentage is your choice.** The tool page is explicit that there is no single rule: the right percentage varies by country, service and custom, and the tool does the maths while the percentage remains your decision.

## Frequently asked questions

**How do I calculate a tip by hand?**
Multiply the bill by the tip percentage divided by 100. For example, a 15% tip on 4,000 is 600, making the total 4,600. Then divide the total by the number of people for each share.

**What tip percentage should I choose?**
There is no single rule — it varies by country, service and custom. The tool does the maths; the percentage is your choice. Pick the rate that matches the service you received and the custom where you are eating.

**Should I tip on the pre-tax or post-tax amount?**
Either is common. Be consistent, and say which one you used if others are splitting with you, so everyone is calculating from the same base.

**Can the calculator split a bill unevenly?**
No — it gives an equal split. For an uneven split, calculate each person's share separately rather than using the equal-split figure.
