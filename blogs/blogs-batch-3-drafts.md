Every cheque has the amount written twice: once in figures and once in words. The words are not decoration. They are the written record of what you meant to pay, and if the two versions ever disagree, the bank will not guess — the cheque is normally returned unpaid and you have to write a fresh one. Writing the amount in words correctly takes ten seconds of care, and most rejections happen for a handful of small, avoidable mistakes.

### The basic rules

Start writing at the very beginning of the line, with no gap before the first word. A space left at the start is space someone else could use to add a word. Write in clear, simple handwriting, in one go, without overwriting or crossing anything out — an altered cheque is a rejected cheque, so if you make a mistake, cancel it and write a new one. End the amount with the word "Only" and draw a line through any space that is left on the line, so nothing can be added after it. For extra safety on larger payments, cross the cheque by writing "A/C Payee" between two lines in the top corner, which means the money can only go into the named person's bank account.

### Lakh and crore, not millions

Cheques in Pakistan and India use the lakh and crore system, so amounts are spoken differently than in the million-based system. One hundred thousand rupees is "One Lakh", not "One Hundred Thousand", and ten million is "One Crore". If you check your amount with a converter, make sure it is set to the right system before you copy the wording — the same number produces different words in the two systems.

### Common mistakes that get cheques returned

- **Words and figures do not match.** This is the most common reason of all. Always compare the two after writing.
- **Overwriting or correcting a word.** Banks do not accept altered cheques. A fresh cheque is faster than an argument at the counter.
- **A gap before the first word, or space left after "Only".** Both invite additions. Start at the edge and close the line.
- **Forgetting "Only".** Without it, an extra word can be added after your amount.
- **Using the wrong numbering system.** 100,000 written as "One Hundred Thousand" on a lakh-system cheque invites confusion; write "One Lakh".
- **Spelling a large amount from memory.** One wrong word in a long amount is easy to miss. Convert it, then copy it.

### Frequently asked questions

**What happens if the amount in words and figures is different?**
The bank will normally return the cheque unpaid rather than decide which amount you meant. Write a fresh cheque with both versions matching exactly.

**Do I have to write "Rupees" and "Only"?**
"Rupees" at the start and "Only" at the end are the standard cheque format and are worth keeping — "Only" in particular closes the amount so nothing can be added after it.

**How do I write an amount with paisa?**
Write the rupees first, then "and", then the paisa: 1,500.50 becomes "Rupees One Thousand Five Hundred and Fifty Paisa Only".
