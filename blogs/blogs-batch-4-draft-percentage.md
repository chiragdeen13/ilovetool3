How to Calculate Percentage — All Three Types, With Worked Examples

Almost every percentage question in daily life is one of three types — and most wrong answers come from using one type's formula on another type's question. "What is 20% of 1,500?", "45 is what percent of 180?", and "sales went from 80 to 100 — what is the percentage increase?" look similar, but each is solved differently. This guide separates the three types, gives the formula for each with a worked example, and shows where our [free Percentage Calculator](https://ilovetool3.com/percentage-calculator/) does the work for you.

## Type 1: What is X% of Y?

**Formula: Y × (X ÷ 100)**

This is the most common type: discounts, tips, tax amounts, interest. You have a total, and you want a stated percentage of it.

Example: what is 20% of 1,500? By hand: 1,500 × (20 ÷ 100) = 1,500 × 0.20 = **300**. We ran exactly this in the [Percentage Calculator](https://ilovetool3.com/percentage-calculator/) and it returned "20% of 1500 = 300" — this first type is precisely what the tool is built for, so for Type 1 questions you never need the formula at all unless you are checking your understanding.

A useful shortcut for mental maths: find 10% first (just move the decimal point one place left — 10% of 1,500 is 150), then double it for 20%, halve it for 5%, and build from there.

## Type 2: X is what percent of Y?

**Formula: (X ÷ Y) × 100**

Here you have a part and a total, and you want to express the part as a percentage. Exam marks are the everyday example: "I scored 378 out of 450 — what percent is that?"

Example 1: 45 is what percent of 180? By hand: 45 ÷ 180 = 0.25, × 100 = **25%**.
Example 2 (marks): 378 ÷ 450 = 0.84, × 100 = **84%**.

Our calculator does not have a separate mode for this type — but there is a neat way to check your answer with it: if you calculate that 45 is 25% of 180, ask the tool for 25% of 180. It returns 45, so your Type 2 answer was right. Checking a division with a multiplication is a habit worth keeping for all three types.

## Type 3: Percentage increase or decrease

**Formula: ((new − old) ÷ old) × 100**

This type compares two values, and the one rule that matters is: **always divide by the old (starting) value**, never by the new one.

Example: a price rises from 80 to 100. Change = 100 − 80 = 20. Then 20 ÷ 80 = 0.25, × 100 = **+25% increase**.

Now the trap — run it backwards. A price falls from 100 to 80. The change is the same 20, but now the starting value is 100: 20 ÷ 100 = **−20% decrease**. The same 20-rupee move is +25% in one direction and −20% in the other, because the base changed. This asymmetry surprises people every day: if your marks drop 20% one term, they must rise 25% the next term just to get back to where you started.

## Common mistakes to avoid

- **Dividing by the new value in change problems.** Going from 80 to 100 is a 25% increase, not 20% — the base is the starting value, 80. This is the most common percentage error in news, business and school alike.
- **Confusing percentage points with percent.** If an interest rate rises from 5% to 7%, that is a rise of 2 percentage points — but a 40% relative increase ((7 − 5) ÷ 5 = 0.40). Both statements are true; they answer different questions, and swapping them misleads.
- **Adding successive discounts.** "20% off, then another 10% off" is not 30% off. On Rs 100: 20% off leaves 80, and 10% off 80 leaves 72 — a total saving of 28%, because the second discount applies to the reduced price.
- **Forgetting the × 100.** 45 ÷ 180 gives 0.25, which is the fraction, not the percentage. The answer in percent is 25% — the final multiplication is what turns one into the other.

## Frequently asked questions

**How do I calculate the percentage of my marks?**
Divide the marks you obtained by the total marks, then multiply by 100. For example, 378 out of 450: 378 ÷ 450 × 100 = 84%.

**What is the formula to find a percentage of a number?**
Multiply the number by the percentage and divide by 100: 20% of 1,500 = 1,500 × 20 ÷ 100 = 300. Or skip the arithmetic and use the [Percentage Calculator](https://ilovetool3.com/percentage-calculator/), which is built for exactly this.

**How do I calculate a percentage increase?**
Subtract the old value from the new value, divide the difference by the old value, and multiply by 100. From 80 to 100: (100 − 80) ÷ 80 × 100 = +25%.
