How to Calculate Your GPA — With a Worked Example on the 4.0 Scale

Your GPA follows you through admissions, scholarships and first job applications — yet most students only ever see it on a transcript, without knowing how it was worked out. The calculation is simple, and almost every wrong GPA comes from one mistake: forgetting that courses with more credit hours count more. This guide explains the formula and the 4.0 scale, with a worked example we ran on our own [free GPA Calculator](https://ilovetool3.com/gpa-calculator/) so you can check the result yourself.

## What GPA actually means

GPA stands for grade point average: the average of the grade points you earned in one term or semester, weighted by credit hours. A 3-credit course moves your GPA three times as much as a 1-credit course with the same grade, because it represents roughly three times the teaching hours. Without that weighting, a top grade in a minor elective would cancel out a weak grade in your main subject.

## The 4.0 scale and grade points

Before any averaging can happen, each letter grade is converted into points. On the most widely used 4.0 scale, the values are:

- A = 4.0
- A- = 3.7
- B+ = 3.3
- B = 3.0
- B- = 2.7
- C+ = 2.3
- C = 2.0
- C- = 1.7
- D = 1.0
- F = 0.0

Two honest warnings. Institutions do not all weight plus and minus grades identically — some count a B+ as 3.3, others as 3.4 — so check your university's official scale for the figure on your record. And our calculator does not display these values next to the grades; it simply does the weighted maths when you press Calculate GPA. The table is the standard scale it is built around.

## The formula

GPA = total grade points ÷ total credit hours

Each course's grade points = its grade value × its credit hours. So the method is always three steps: convert every grade to points, multiply by that course's credit hours and add them up, then divide by the total credit hours. Everything else in this article is that formula applied carefully.

## A worked example, tested on the tool

Take a student with three courses in a semester:

- Course 1: grade A, 3 credit hours
- Course 2: grade B+, 3 credit hours
- Course 3: grade A-, 2 credit hours

By hand, on the standard scale: A is worth 4.0 × 3 = 12.0 points; B+ is worth 3.3 × 3 = 9.9; A- is worth 3.7 × 2 = 7.4. Total grade points = 12.0 + 9.9 + 7.4 = 29.3. Total credit hours = 3 + 3 + 2 = 8. So GPA = 29.3 ÷ 8 = 3.66.

We entered exactly these courses into the [GPA Calculator](https://ilovetool3.com/gpa-calculator/), and it returned "Your GPA: 3.66" with "Total credit hours: 8" — hand calculation and tool agree, which is exactly the check to run with any calculator, including ours. Notice the weighting: the plain average of the three grade values (4.0, 3.3, 3.7) is 3.67, but because the A- course carried only 2 credits, the true result is 3.66. When credit hours differ more, that gap grows.

## GPA is not CGPA

Your GPA describes one semester; your CGPA is the cumulative figure across every semester so far — the same weighted calculation stretched over all your courses. One strong semester does not rewrite a weak record, and one bad semester does not erase several good ones. ## Common mistakes to avoid

- **Averaging the letters directly.** Adding grade values and dividing by the number of courses ignores credit hours, and quietly gives the wrong answer whenever credits differ.
- **Assuming every university uses the same scale.** Plus and minus values vary between institutions, and some schools do not use them at all. Your transcript follows your institution's scale, not a generic table.
- **Leaving out a failed course.** An F earns 0 points, but its credit hours still sit in the total and pull the GPA down. If you retake a course, whether the old grade is replaced, averaged or kept depends on your institution's retake rule — check it before relying on any estimate.

## Frequently asked questions

**What is a good GPA?**
It depends on what you need it for. A 3.0 is a solid B average and meets the minimum for many purposes; competitive scholarships, graduate programmes and employers often look for 3.5 or above. The threshold set by the specific institution or employer is the one that matters.

**How do I convert my GPA to a percentage?**
There is no universal formula. A rough estimate on the 4.0 scale is GPA ÷ 4 × 100, but many institutions use their own conversion or none at all. For a CGPA on a 10-point scale, use the [CGPA to Percentage calculator](https://ilovetool3.com/cgpa-to-percentage/) — and for anything official, confirm your institution's method.

**Does the calculator save my grades?**
No. The calculation runs in your browser and your entries are not saved or sent anywhere, so once you close the page, nothing is kept.

**Can I calculate my CGPA with this tool?**
It is built for one term's GPA. The same weighted maths stretches to a CGPA if you enter every course from every semester, but most students find it easier to calculate each semester separately, then combine the semester GPAs weighted by their credit hours.
