Income Tax Slabs in Pakistan 2026-27 for Salaried Persons — Rates and Examples

The tax year 2026-27 began on 1 July 2026, and with it a revised set of income tax slabs for salaried persons in Pakistan. If your salary has changed — or you simply want to know why the deduction on your payslip is what it is — this guide gives you the full 2026-27 slab table, explains how slab tax actually works, and walks through examples we verified on our own [free Income Tax Calculator](https://ilovetool3.com/tax-calculator/). Most people think in monthly salary, so every example gives the monthly figure as well.

## The full slab table for 2026-27 (salaried persons)

- Annual salary up to Rs 600,000: **0%** — no tax
- Rs 600,001 to Rs 1,200,000: **1%** of the amount above Rs 600,000
- Rs 1,200,001 to Rs 2,200,000: **Rs 6,000 + 11%** of the amount above Rs 1,200,000
- Rs 2,200,001 to Rs 3,200,000: **Rs 116,000 + 20%** of the amount above Rs 2,200,000
- Rs 3,200,001 to Rs 4,100,000: **Rs 316,000 + 25%** of the amount above Rs 3,200,000
- Rs 4,100,001 to Rs 5,600,000: **Rs 541,000 + 29%** of the amount above Rs 4,100,000
- Rs 5,600,001 to Rs 7,000,000: **Rs 976,000 + 32%** of the amount above Rs 5,600,000
- Above Rs 7,000,000: **Rs 1,424,000 + 35%** of the amount above Rs 7,000,000

This table is for salaried individuals only — the rates for business income and for companies are different. One change from the previous year worth knowing: the additional surcharge that used to apply on top of the tax for very high incomes is not part of the 2026-27 rates.

## How slab tax actually works

Slab tax is progressive: your income is sliced into bands, and only the slice inside each band is taxed at that band's rate. This is the single most misunderstood point in the whole system. Crossing into a higher slab never taxes your whole salary at the higher rate — only the part above the threshold.

Take an annual salary of Rs 2,000,000. The first Rs 600,000 is tax-free. The next Rs 600,000 (up to 1,200,000) is taxed at 1%, which is Rs 6,000. Only the remaining Rs 800,000 falls in the 11% band, which is Rs 88,000. Total tax: Rs 94,000. That is an effective rate of 4.70% on the whole salary — nowhere near 11%, even though 11% is the salary's top, or marginal, rate. When you judge your own tax bill, the effective rate is the honest number; the marginal rate only tells you what the next rupee earned will be taxed at.

## Common mistakes and misunderstandings

- **"My whole salary will be taxed at the new rate."** No — as the Rs 2,000,000 example shows, only the amount above each threshold is taxed at the higher rate. A small raise that pushes you into a new slab still leaves you better off.
- **Entering a monthly salary into an annual field.** If you type Rs 100,000 as your "annual" income, the calculator quite correctly shows Rs 0 tax — because a Rs 100,000 yearly salary is tax-free. Always convert to the full year first.
- **Using the salaried table for other income.** These slabs are for salary. Business income, and individuals whose income is mainly non-salary, are taxed under a different table, and companies under another. Mixing them up produces confident, wrong numbers.
- **Treating the estimate as the final liability.** Deductions, exemptions, tax credits and income from other sources all change the final figure, and your employer's withholding is adjusted against your annual return. The calculator follows the slab table only.

## Frequently asked questions

**How much salary is tax-free in Pakistan in 2026-27?**
A salaried person pays no income tax on annual salary up to Rs 600,000 — that is Rs 50,000 a month.

**What is the tax on a Rs 100,000 monthly salary?**
Rs 100,000 a month is Rs 1,200,000 a year, which gives a tax of Rs 6,000 for the whole year — about Rs 500 a month, an effective rate of 0.50%.

**What is the tax on a Rs 250,000 monthly salary?**
Rs 250,000 a month is Rs 3,000,000 a year. Tax is Rs 276,000 for the year (Rs 23,000 a month), an effective rate of 9.20% — verified on the calculator above.

**Does my employer deduct this tax?**
Generally, yes: salary tax is withheld by the employer in monthly instalments based on your expected annual salary, and reconciled when you file your annual return. If your income changes mid-year, the monthly deduction changes with it.
